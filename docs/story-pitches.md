# Story pitches (for you to pick)

The brief asks for 2-3 concepts that remix Red's story around Resonance, with a
rival who takes the opposite approach. Nothing below is built. Each one reuses
Red's maps and beats where it can, so it stays in reach of the engine.

## 1. The Forced Bond

Team Rocket has learned that Resonance can be *forced*: Silph Co.'s "Sync
Collar" drags a Pokemon into Resonance with a trainer whether it trusts them or
not. The collars work, and they break the Pokemon.

- **Beats:**
  - The Pokemon Tower ghosts are collared Pokemon that were worn out.
  - The Silph Co. takeover is Rocket seizing collar production.
  - Mewtwo is the first thing a collar was ever made for.
- **The rival** buys the collars. They're genuinely winning with them, and
  your fights are "bond vs power" in mechanics too: their team resonates at
  every gym, and pays for it with a stunned turn you can exploit.
- **Ending:** you calm Mewtwo (a Frenzy boss) instead of catching it. The
  collar comes off only if its trust meter, driven by your Bond play, fills
  before your trainer HP runs out.
- **Uses:** Resonance, the Dodge Phase, Frenzy bosses, Bond, the Mercy option.

## 2. The Last Resonant

Resonance was common a century ago and has faded. Professor Oak thinks your
starter is the first Pokemon in decades to reach it, and every gym leader is
secretly a keeper of one piece of the old technique.

- **Beats:**
  - Each badge comes with a lesson that unlocks a Bond technique: Brock
    teaches Clash, Misty the Improvise, and so on.
  - The rival is Oak's grandchild, sure they were owed the gift, who turns to
    Giovanni's "science" to catch up.
  - The hidden legendaries are the last wild Resonant Pokemon.
- **Ending:** the rival at the League with a Pokemon that resonates through
  fear, not trust. The post-game Mt. Silver-style fight is against a trainer
  from the old age.
- **Uses:** gyms as teachers (fits the any-order open world), Improvise,
  techniques, the calendar (lessons on certain days).

## 3. Two Frequencies

Every Pokemon has a frequency. Bond tunes you to it; Rocket's broadcast tower
in Lavender (the Pokemon Tower, repurposed) jams it, and wild Pokemon across
Kanto are turning frantic.

- **Beats:**
  - Frenzy bosses are Pokemon driven mad by the broadcast: Snorlax on the
    road, Onix in Rock Tunnel, the three birds.
  - Calming them, not beating them, restores each region and opens its paths.
    That's the open world's gate logic.
  - The rival resonates by "overdriving": Bond is raw power to them, and they
    push their Pokemon past the break.
- **Ending:** you shut the tower down in a Dodge Phase gauntlet. Mewtwo was
  the broadcast's source, and you calm it last.
- **Uses:** Frenzy bosses as the spine of the open world, the choices-matter
  brief ("calm or defeat changes later dialogue"), and Field States (the jam
  as a battle environment).

**My pick, if you want one:** 3. It gives the open world its reason (calming
regions opens paths), puts Frenzy bosses at the centre instead of the edge,
and its rival mirrors Resonance's shared-pain rule directly.
